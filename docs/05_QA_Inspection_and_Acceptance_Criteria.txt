DOCUMENT TITLE: QA Inspection and Acceptance Criteria
DOCUMENT ID: QA-SOP-005
VERSION: 1.0
STATUS: Approved for Training / Demo Use
PROCESS OWNER: Quality Assurance
REVIEW FREQUENCY: Annual

1. PURPOSE

This procedure defines the minimum requirements for QA/QC inspection, verification, acceptance, rejection, rework, and release decisions.

The objective is to ensure that products, services, records, documents, and process outputs are accepted only when defined requirements are met and sufficient evidence is available.

2. SCOPE

This procedure applies to:

- QA inspections
- QC inspections
- Process verification
- Document verification
- Record review
- Deliverable acceptance
- Rework verification
- Final release checks
- Quality-control checkpoints

3. INSPECTION REQUIREMENTS

An inspection must be performed against defined requirements.

Inspection criteria may include:

- Approved specification
- Controlled procedure
- Checklist
- Acceptance criteria
- Contractual requirement
- Approved drawing or document
- Required approval
- Mandatory record requirement

The inspector must not create new acceptance criteria during the inspection.

4. REQUIRED INSPECTION EVIDENCE

Where applicable, inspection evidence should include:

- Inspection date
- Inspector name
- Item or process inspected
- Requirement checked
- Result
- Supporting record or reference
- Exceptions identified
- Approval or release status

Inspection evidence must be sufficient to demonstrate why an item was accepted or rejected.

5. ACCEPTANCE DECISION

A result may be classified as ACCEPTED when:

- All mandatory requirements are met.
- Required evidence is available.
- Required approvals are complete.
- No unresolved critical defect exists.
- Required inspection steps were completed.
- Any permitted deviation has documented approval.

Acceptance must be evidence-based.

6. CONDITIONAL ACCEPTANCE

Conditional acceptance may be used only when:

- The applicable process allows it.
- Remaining issue does not create significant quality, safety, regulatory, contractual, or customer risk.
- Required approval for conditional acceptance is documented.
- Open actions have a responsible owner and due date.

Conditional acceptance without required approval is not valid.

7. REJECTION

A result must be rejected when:

- A mandatory requirement is not met.
- Required inspection was not completed.
- Critical evidence is missing.
- Required approval is unavailable.
- A critical defect exists.
- The result creates significant customer, regulatory, contractual, safety, or quality risk.

Rejected output must not proceed to final release unless formally corrected and reverified.

8. REWORK

Rework may be required when a defect can be corrected without creating unacceptable risk.

Rework requirements include:

- Defect clearly identified
- Required correction defined
- Responsible person assigned
- Rework completed
- Reinspection performed
- Final result documented

Rework does not automatically close the related NCR if a Non-Conformance was already raised.

9. REINSPECTION

Reinspection is required when:

- Rework was performed
- A rejected item was corrected
- A critical issue was resolved
- Previous evidence was incomplete
- Corrective action requires verification

Reinspection must confirm compliance with the original approved acceptance criteria.

10. MISSING INSPECTION EVIDENCE

Missing required inspection evidence is a quality issue.

Classification should consider:

A. Minor NCR may apply when:
- The inspection was actually performed.
- Supporting evidence can be reliably reconstructed.
- No significant risk exists.
- The issue is isolated.

B. Major NCR may apply when:
- Required inspection was not performed.
- Evidence cannot demonstrate compliance.
- Multiple records are affected.
- Critical release control was bypassed.
- Customer, contractual, regulatory, or safety risk exists.

Final classification must follow the Non-Conformance Procedure and Severity Classification Matrix.

11. DEFECT SEVERITY

Inspection findings may be evaluated as:

CRITICAL DEFECT
A defect that may create safety, regulatory, contractual, significant customer, or severe quality risk.

MAJOR DEFECT
A defect that prevents required functionality, compliance, or acceptance but does not meet the Critical definition.

MINOR DEFECT
A limited defect that does not significantly affect intended use or compliance but still requires correction where applicable.

Defect severity and NCR severity are related but are not automatically identical.

12. RELEASE CONTROL

Final release should occur only after:

- Required inspection is complete.
- Acceptance criteria are met.
- Mandatory approvals are complete.
- Required records are available.
- Open critical issues are resolved.
- Required NCR/CAPA actions do not prevent release.

Unauthorized release before required QA/QC approval is a serious control failure.

13. SAMPLING

Sampling may be used when full inspection is not required by the applicable procedure.

Sampling decisions should consider:

- Risk level
- Process history
- Previous defects
- Record volume
- Customer requirement
- Contractual requirement
- Regulatory requirement

Sampling must not be used to avoid a mandatory 100% inspection requirement.

14. INSPECTION CHECKLIST CONTROL

Inspection checklists must be:

- Approved
- Current
- Applicable to the activity
- Completed accurately
- Traceable to the inspector
- Retained as quality evidence where required

Use of an obsolete or unauthorized checklist must be evaluated under the Document Control and Non-Conformance procedures.

15. ESCALATION

Escalation to QA Management is required when:

- Critical defect is identified
- Mandatory inspection was bypassed
- Unauthorized release occurred
- Required evidence cannot demonstrate compliance
- Multiple failures are identified
- Repeated inspection failures occur
- Significant customer impact exists
- Regulatory or contractual risk exists

16. INSPECTION RECORD STATUS

Recommended statuses include:

- Pending Inspection
- Accepted
- Conditionally Accepted
- Rejected
- Rework Required
- Reinspection Required
- Released
- Hold

17. RAG COPILOT DECISION RULE

For inspection-related questions, the QA/QC RAG Copilot must:

- Identify the applicable inspection or acceptance requirement.
- Determine whether sufficient evidence exists.
- Identify whether the result should be Accepted, Conditionally Accepted, Rejected, Rework Required, or Manual QA Review.
- Distinguish an inspection result from an NCR classification.
- Use the Non-Conformance Procedure when a requirement has been violated.
- Recommend escalation when supported by documented rules.
- Identify the source document used.
- Never invent inspection results, approvals, defects, or supporting evidence.

If sufficient evidence is unavailable, respond:

MANUAL QA REVIEW REQUIRED

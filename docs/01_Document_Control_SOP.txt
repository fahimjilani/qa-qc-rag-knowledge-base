DOCUMENT TITLE: Document Control SOP
DOCUMENT ID: QA-SOP-001
VERSION: 1.0
STATUS: Approved for Training / Demo Use
PROCESS OWNER: Quality Assurance
REVIEW FREQUENCY: Annual

1. PURPOSE

This procedure defines how controlled QA/QC documents, forms, procedures, work instructions, checklists, templates, and records are created, reviewed, approved, issued, revised, distributed, and withdrawn.

The objective is to ensure employees use only the current approved version of a controlled document.

2. SCOPE

This procedure applies to:

- QA and QC procedures
- Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs)
- Work instructions
- Inspection checklists
- Controlled forms and templates
- Audit documents
- CAPA forms
- NCR forms
- Approval matrices
- Quality records

3. DOCUMENT CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

3.1 Every controlled document must have:

- Unique document ID
- Document title
- Version or revision number
- Document owner
- Approval status
- Effective date

3.2 A controlled document must not be released for operational use until all required approvals are completed.

3.3 Draft documents must be clearly identified as DRAFT and must not be used for official operational activities.

3.4 Only the latest approved version may be used for operational activities.

3.5 Obsolete versions must be removed from active-use locations or clearly marked OBSOLETE.

4. DOCUMENT CREATION AND APPROVAL

The document owner prepares or revises the document.

Required reviewers verify:

- Technical accuracy
- Process compliance
- Regulatory or contractual requirements, where applicable
- Correct document format
- Required approval authority

The document becomes controlled only after the required approver authorizes it.

5. DOCUMENT REVISION

Every revision must include:

- New revision number
- Description of change
- Date of revision
- Reviewer details
- Approver details

Employees must not manually modify an approved controlled document without following the formal revision process.

6. CONTROLLED FORM REQUIREMENTS

Controlled forms must be used exactly as approved.

The following are not allowed without authorization:

- Removing mandatory fields
- Changing approval sections
- Editing controlled instructions
- Creating unofficial copies for operational use
- Using an outdated form
- Using an unapproved replacement form

7. UNCONTROLLED OR UNAPPROVED DOCUMENT USE

Use of an unapproved or obsolete controlled document is a quality issue.

The issue must be evaluated based on impact.

Examples:

A. Minor Non-Conformance may apply when:
- An obsolete form was used but no required control was bypassed.
- The issue was isolated.
- No customer, compliance, product, service, or record integrity impact occurred.
- The correct information can be fully reconstructed.

B. Major Non-Conformance may apply when:
- An unapproved document bypassed a mandatory approval.
- Required quality evidence cannot be demonstrated.
- Multiple records are affected.
- The issue creates regulatory, contractual, customer, safety, or significant quality risk.
- An obsolete procedure resulted in an incorrect operational decision.
- Required records cannot be reconstructed.

Final classification must also follow the NCR Severity Classification Matrix.

8. DOCUMENT DISTRIBUTION

Controlled documents may be distributed through approved systems such as:

- Document Management System
- Controlled shared repository
- Approved quality portal

Email attachments or locally saved copies should not be treated as the master controlled version unless specifically authorized.

9. OBSOLETE DOCUMENT CONTROL

When a document is replaced:

- The previous version must be withdrawn from active use.
- Electronic access should be restricted where appropriate.
- Archived versions must remain identifiable.
- The effective version must be clearly available.

10. DOCUMENT CONTROL VIOLATIONS

Examples of violations include:

- Using an unapproved SOP
- Using an obsolete checklist
- Changing an approved form without authorization
- Missing approval evidence
- Unauthorized document distribution
- Failure to remove obsolete documents
- Operational use of a draft document

These cases must be evaluated through the Non-Conformance Procedure.

11. CORRECTIVE ACTION

Depending on severity, corrective action may include:

- Immediate withdrawal of the incorrect document
- Replacement with the current approved version
- Review of affected records
- Retraining of responsible personnel
- Root cause analysis
- CAPA initiation
- Verification that obsolete copies have been removed

12. ESCALATION

Escalation to QA Management is required when:

- A Major Non-Conformance is suspected.
- Multiple departments are affected.
- Customer or regulatory impact may exist.
- Required approval was deliberately bypassed.
- Records cannot demonstrate compliance.
- The same document-control failure has occurred repeatedly.

13. RECORDS

The following evidence should be retained where applicable:

- Approval history
- Revision history
- Distribution records
- Obsolete-document archive
- Training acknowledgement
- NCR reference
- CAPA reference

14. DECISION RULE

If the available evidence is insufficient to determine whether document-control requirements were violated, the case must be referred for manual QA review.

The QA/QC RAG Copilot must not invent approvals, requirements, document versions, or classifications that are not supported by the approved knowledge base.

END OF DOCUMENT

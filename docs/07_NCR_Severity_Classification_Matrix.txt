DOCUMENT TITLE: NCR Severity Classification Matrix
DOCUMENT ID: QA-SOP-007
VERSION: 1.0
STATUS: Approved for Training / Demo Use
PROCESS OWNER: Quality Assurance
REVIEW FREQUENCY: Annual

1. PURPOSE

This document provides a structured severity matrix for classifying QA/QC findings and Non-Conformances.

The objective is to support consistent classification using impact, recurrence, scope, evidence availability, and control failure severity.

2. CLASSIFICATION CATEGORIES

Findings must be classified as:

- Compliant
- Observation
- Minor Non-Conformance
- Major Non-Conformance
- Manual QA Review Required

3. SEVERITY FACTORS

The following factors must be considered:

A. CUSTOMER IMPACT

0 = No customer impact
1 = Limited internal impact only
2 = Potential customer impact
3 = Confirmed customer impact

B. REGULATORY / CONTRACTUAL IMPACT

0 = None
1 = Low or indirect risk
2 = Potential compliance exposure
3 = Confirmed regulatory or contractual breach

C. SAFETY IMPACT

0 = No safety impact
1 = Low potential risk
2 = Moderate safety concern
3 = Significant or critical safety concern

D. CONTROL FAILURE

0 = No control failure
1 = Partial control weakness
2 = Mandatory control partially bypassed
3 = Mandatory or critical control completely bypassed

E. SCOPE

0 = Single isolated item
1 = Small number of records
2 = Multiple records or one department
3 = Multiple departments or systemic scope

F. RECURRENCE

0 = First occurrence
1 = Similar previous issue
2 = Repeated issue
3 = Repeated issue after prior corrective action or CAPA

G. EVIDENCE AVAILABILITY

0 = Complete evidence available
1 = Minor evidence gap
2 = Important evidence incomplete
3 = Required compliance evidence unavailable

4. SEVERITY SCORE

Severity Score is calculated by adding the applicable factor scores.

Minimum score: 0
Maximum score: 21

5. SCORE INTERPRETATION

0–2:
COMPLIANT or OBSERVATION

Typical characteristics:
- No confirmed mandatory requirement failure
- Low or no risk
- Improvement opportunity only

3–6:
MINOR NON-CONFORMANCE

Typical characteristics:
- Isolated failure
- Limited impact
- No significant regulatory, contractual, customer, or safety risk
- Evidence can be reconstructed
- No systemic weakness

7–12:
MAJOR NON-CONFORMANCE

Typical characteristics:
- Significant process or control failure
- Multiple affected records
- Mandatory approval bypassed
- Important evidence unavailable
- Repeated issue
- Customer, contractual, or regulatory risk may exist

13–21:
MAJOR NON-CONFORMANCE – HIGH PRIORITY

Typical characteristics:
- Systemic or widespread failure
- Critical control bypass
- Confirmed customer or compliance impact
- Significant safety risk
- Repeated ineffective corrective action
- Immediate management escalation required

6. OVERRIDE RULES

A finding may be classified as Major NCR regardless of total score when any of the following occurs:

- Mandatory approval completely bypassed
- Critical inspection skipped
- Regulatory breach confirmed
- Significant safety risk identified
- Required compliance evidence unavailable
- Unauthorized final release occurred
- Same issue recurred after CAPA
- Multiple departments are affected by the same systemic failure

7. OBSERVATION RULE

An Observation must not be used when a mandatory requirement has clearly been violated.

Observation is appropriate only when:

- No confirmed mandatory requirement failure exists
- Improvement opportunity is identified
- Future risk may exist
- Current control remains compliant

8. MINOR NCR RULE

Minor NCR is appropriate when:

- Requirement was not fully met
- Issue is isolated
- Scope is limited
- No significant customer, regulatory, contractual, or safety impact exists
- Evidence remains available
- No systemic or repeated failure exists

9. MAJOR NCR RULE

Major NCR is appropriate when:

- Mandatory control was bypassed
- Significant evidence is missing
- Multiple records are affected
- Same failure is repeated
- Systemic weakness exists
- Customer, regulatory, contractual, or safety risk exists
- Previous corrective action was ineffective

10. MANUAL QA REVIEW RULE

Manual QA Review is required when:

- Facts are incomplete
- Impact cannot be determined
- Evidence conflicts
- Severity factors cannot be reliably scored
- Required source documents are unavailable
- User description is too vague to support classification

The RAG Copilot must not guess missing facts.

11. ESCALATION RULE

Escalation is mandatory for:

- Major NCR
- High-Priority Major NCR
- Regulatory or contractual risk
- Safety-related risk
- Repeated NCR
- Systemic issue
- Missing critical compliance evidence
- Ineffective previous CAPA

12. CAPA TRIGGER

CAPA should be recommended when:

- Severity score is 7 or higher
- Major NCR is confirmed
- Repeat Minor NCR indicates systemic weakness
- Previous corrective action failed
- Significant customer, compliance, or safety risk exists

13. RAG COPILOT SCORING RULE

The QA/QC RAG Copilot may recommend a severity score only when sufficient evidence exists for the individual factors.

The response should identify:

- Classification
- Severity Score
- Main severity factors
- Requirement violated
- Recommended corrective action
- Escalation requirement
- CAPA recommendation
- Source document

If a factor cannot be determined from the available evidence, the Copilot must not invent a value.

If missing information could materially change the classification, respond:

MANUAL QA REVIEW REQUIRED

END OF DOCUMENT

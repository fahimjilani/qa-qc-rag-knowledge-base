DOCUMENT TITLE: Non-Conformance Procedure
DOCUMENT ID: QA-SOP-002
VERSION: 1.0
STATUS: Approved for Training / Demo Use
PROCESS OWNER: Quality Assurance
REVIEW FREQUENCY: Annual

1. PURPOSE

This procedure defines how quality non-conformances are identified, evaluated, classified, recorded, investigated, corrected, escalated, and closed.

The objective is to ensure that quality issues are handled consistently based on risk, impact, recurrence, and evidence.

2. SCOPE

This procedure applies to:

- QA and QC findings
- Internal audit findings
- Process deviations
- Document-control failures
- Inspection failures
- Approval-control failures
- Repeated quality issues
- Customer-impacting quality failures
- Compliance-related issues
- Corrective and preventive action triggers

3. DEFINITION OF NON-CONFORMANCE

A Non-Conformance occurs when a documented requirement, approved procedure, specification, control, approval rule, or acceptance criterion has not been met.

A finding must be supported by objective evidence.

Examples include:

- Required approval missing
- Approved procedure not followed
- Incorrect or obsolete controlled document used
- Required inspection not performed
- Mandatory record missing
- Acceptance criteria not met
- Unauthorized process change
- Repeated failure of a defined control

4. CLASSIFICATION CATEGORIES

Every finding must be classified as one of the following:

A. COMPLIANT

Use when:

- The required procedure was followed.
- Required approvals are available.
- Required records are complete.
- Acceptance criteria are met.
- No deviation from an approved requirement is identified.

No NCR is required.

B. OBSERVATION

An Observation is a condition that does not currently represent a confirmed breach of a mandatory requirement but may create future quality risk.

Examples:

- Improvement opportunity
- Minor documentation clarity issue
- Potential weakness with no confirmed control failure
- Good practice not consistently followed where it is not mandatory

Observations should be monitored but do not automatically require an NCR.

C. MINOR NON-CONFORMANCE

A Minor Non-Conformance is an isolated failure that does not create significant quality, regulatory, contractual, safety, or customer risk.

Minor NCR indicators include:

- Isolated incident
- Limited scope
- Required control existed but was partially followed
- Records can be reconstructed
- No significant customer impact
- No regulatory impact
- No systemic failure
- No repeated pattern

Examples:

- One obsolete form used with no process impact
- One approval completed late but before final release
- Minor record-entry omission that can be verified
- Single procedural deviation with no downstream impact

D. MAJOR NON-CONFORMANCE

A Major Non-Conformance represents a significant or systemic failure.

Major NCR indicators include:

- Mandatory approval bypassed
- Critical quality control not performed
- Required evidence unavailable
- Multiple records affected
- Repeated or systemic issue
- Significant customer impact
- Contractual requirement violated
- Regulatory or legal risk
- Safety-related risk
- Integrity of quality records cannot be demonstrated
- Previous corrective action failed
- Unauthorized process used in production or operational activity

Examples:

- Controlled process released without required approval
- Inspection requirement completely skipped
- Multiple records created using an unapproved procedure
- Repeated NCR for the same root cause
- Missing quality evidence affecting customer delivery

5. CLASSIFICATION RULES

Classification must consider:

- Requirement violated
- Number of records affected
- Frequency of occurrence
- Customer impact
- Regulatory impact
- Contractual impact
- Safety impact
- Ability to reconstruct records
- Whether the issue is isolated or systemic
- Whether similar NCRs occurred previously

When evidence is insufficient, the system must not guess.

The case must be marked:

MANUAL QA REVIEW REQUIRED

6. IMMEDIATE CONTAINMENT

When a Non-Conformance is identified, immediate containment may include:

- Stop use of incorrect document or process
- Quarantine affected records or output
- Prevent further processing
- Correct obvious data errors
- Inform responsible process owner
- Replace obsolete documentation
- Preserve evidence for investigation

Containment is not the same as corrective action.

7. ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS

Root cause analysis is required when:

- The NCR is classified as Major
- The issue is repeated
- The issue indicates systemic weakness
- Management requests investigation
- Customer, contractual, or regulatory impact exists

Acceptable root cause methods may include:

- 5 Whys
- Fishbone analysis
- Process review
- Control failure analysis
- Evidence review

8. CORRECTIVE ACTION

Corrective actions must address the identified root cause.

Examples:

- Procedure revision
- Additional approval control
- System validation rule
- Retraining
- Access-control change
- Checklist improvement
- Process redesign
- Additional quality verification
- Document withdrawal

Corrective action must not consist only of correcting the affected record.

9. CAPA REQUIREMENT

A CAPA should be initiated when:

- Major NCR is confirmed
- Same issue has occurred repeatedly
- Root cause indicates systemic failure
- Preventive controls are required
- Customer or compliance risk exists
- Previous corrective action was ineffective

10. ESCALATION RULES

Escalation to QA Management is mandatory when:

- Major NCR is identified
- Regulatory risk exists
- Customer impact exists
- Safety risk exists
- Multiple departments are affected
- Mandatory approval was bypassed
- Evidence cannot demonstrate compliance
- Repeated non-conformance is detected
- NCR remains overdue
- Corrective action is ineffective

Minor NCRs may be handled by the process owner unless escalation is required due to recurrence or risk.

11. NCR RECORD REQUIREMENTS

Every NCR record should contain:

- NCR ID
- Date identified
- Reporter
- Department
- Issue description
- Requirement violated
- Supporting evidence
- Source procedure
- Classification
- Severity
- Containment action
- Corrective action
- Assigned owner
- Due date
- Escalation status
- Closure status

12. CLOSURE REQUIREMENTS

An NCR must not be closed until:

- Required corrective action is completed
- Supporting evidence is available
- Responsible owner confirms completion
- QA verifies effectiveness where required
- Related CAPA is linked if applicable
- No open mandatory actions remain

13. EFFECTIVENESS VERIFICATION

Effectiveness verification is required for:

- Major NCRs
- Repeated Minor NCRs
- CAPA-related findings
- Systemic failures

Verification may include:

- Follow-up audit
- Sample review
- Record inspection
- Process observation
- Repeat-control testing

14. OVERDUE NCRs

An NCR is considered overdue when the defined due date has passed and required actions remain incomplete.

Overdue Major NCRs must be escalated to QA Management.

15. RAG COPILOT DECISION RULE

The QA/QC RAG Copilot must:

- Use only evidence from approved knowledge-base documents.
- Identify the requirement involved.
- Classify the issue as Compliant, Observation, Minor NCR, or Major NCR.
- Explain the reason for the classification.
- Recommend corrective action.
- Determine whether escalation is required.
- Identify the source document used.
- Avoid inventing missing evidence.
- Refer uncertain cases for manual QA review.

END OF DOCUMENT

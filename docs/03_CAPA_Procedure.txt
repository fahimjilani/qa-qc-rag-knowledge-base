DOCUMENT TITLE: Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA) Procedure
DOCUMENT ID: QA-SOP-003
VERSION: 1.0
STATUS: Approved for Training / Demo Use
PROCESS OWNER: Quality Assurance
REVIEW FREQUENCY: Annual

1. PURPOSE

This procedure defines when Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA) is required and how CAPA cases are initiated, investigated, implemented, verified, and closed.

The objective is to eliminate root causes of significant or recurring quality problems and reduce the risk of recurrence.

2. SCOPE

This procedure applies to:

- Major Non-Conformances
- Repeated Minor Non-Conformances
- Internal audit findings
- Customer-impacting quality failures
- Regulatory or contractual quality issues
- Repeated document-control failures
- Systemic process weaknesses
- Failed previous corrective actions
- Significant control breakdowns

3. CAPA TRIGGERS

CAPA must be considered when:

- A Major NCR is confirmed.
- The same or similar issue occurs repeatedly.
- Root cause indicates a systemic weakness.
- Customer impact exists.
- Regulatory or contractual risk exists.
- Safety-related quality risk exists.
- Previous corrective action was ineffective.
- Management requests formal investigation.
- Multiple departments or processes are affected.

CAPA is not automatically required for every isolated Minor NCR.

4. CAPA INITIATION

Every CAPA record should include:

- CAPA ID
- Related NCR ID
- Date initiated
- Problem statement
- Department
- Process owner
- Risk level
- Root cause investigation requirement
- Assigned owner
- Target completion date
- Current status

5. PROBLEM STATEMENT

The problem statement must describe:

- What happened
- Where it happened
- When it happened
- What requirement was not met
- How the issue was detected
- Known impact
- Scope of affected records, products, services, or processes

The problem statement must be factual and evidence-based.

6. ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS

Root cause analysis must identify why the issue occurred.

Acceptable methods may include:

- 5 Whys
- Fishbone / Ishikawa analysis
- Process mapping
- Failure mode review
- Control gap analysis
- Record and evidence review
- Interview of responsible personnel

The investigation must distinguish between:

- Immediate cause
- Contributing factors
- Root cause

7. CORRECTIVE ACTION

Corrective Action addresses the root cause of an existing problem.

Examples include:

- Procedure revision
- Additional approval control
- System validation
- Mandatory retraining
- Access-control change
- Workflow redesign
- Additional inspection step
- Automated validation rule
- Improved document control
- Responsibility reassignment

Correcting an individual record alone does not constitute an adequate corrective action when the root cause remains unresolved.

8. PREVENTIVE ACTION

Preventive Action reduces the likelihood of similar failures occurring elsewhere.

Examples include:

- Updating related procedures
- Expanding control checks
- Additional monitoring
- Preventive training
- System alerts
- Standardized templates
- Cross-department process review

Preventive action should be proportionate to the identified risk.

9. ACTION PLAN REQUIREMENTS

Each action must include:

- Action description
- Responsible owner
- Due date
- Required evidence
- Completion status

Actions must be practical, measurable, and linked to the identified root cause.

10. APPROVAL

CAPA plans associated with Major NCRs must be reviewed or approved by QA Management.

Additional approval may be required when:

- Regulatory impact exists
- Customer commitment is affected
- Multiple departments are involved
- Significant system change is proposed

11. IMPLEMENTATION EVIDENCE

Examples of acceptable evidence include:

- Revised procedure
- Approved workflow change
- Training record
- System configuration evidence
- Updated checklist
- Completed verification report
- Audit evidence
- Management approval

Verbal confirmation alone is not sufficient evidence for CAPA closure.

12. EFFECTIVENESS VERIFICATION

A CAPA must not be closed until effectiveness is verified.

Verification may include:

- Follow-up audit
- Sample review
- Repeat testing
- Trend analysis
- Record inspection
- Process observation
- Control effectiveness testing

Effectiveness verification must determine whether:

- The root cause was addressed.
- The issue has not recurred.
- The new control operates as intended.
- No unacceptable new risk was introduced.

13. INEFFECTIVE CAPA

A CAPA is ineffective when:

- The same issue recurs.
- Required actions were not implemented.
- Root cause was incorrectly identified.
- Evidence does not prove effectiveness.
- Control weakness remains.

An ineffective CAPA must be escalated to QA Management.

The case may require:

- Reopened investigation
- New root cause analysis
- Revised action plan
- Higher-level management review

14. CAPA DUE DATE AND OVERDUE CONTROL

Each CAPA must have an agreed target completion date.

If the due date passes while mandatory actions remain open, the CAPA becomes overdue.

Overdue CAPAs linked to Major NCRs must be escalated.

15. CAPA STATUS

Recommended statuses are:

- Open
- Investigation
- Action Plan Approved
- Implementation in Progress
- Effectiveness Review
- Closed
- Overdue
- Reopened

16. CLOSURE REQUIREMENTS

CAPA may be closed only when:

- Root cause analysis is complete.
- Required actions are complete.
- Supporting evidence is available.
- Effectiveness has been verified.
- Required QA approval is complete.
- No mandatory actions remain open.

17. RAG COPILOT DECISION RULE

The QA/QC RAG Copilot must recommend CAPA when the knowledge-base evidence indicates:

- Major NCR
- Repeated Minor NCR
- Systemic failure
- Significant customer, regulatory, contractual, or safety risk
- Ineffective previous corrective action

If available evidence is insufficient to determine whether CAPA is required, the response must state:

MANUAL QA REVIEW REQUIRED

The RAG Copilot must not invent root causes, approvals, recurrence history, or CAPA evidence.

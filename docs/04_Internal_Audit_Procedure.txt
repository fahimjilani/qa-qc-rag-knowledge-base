PROCESS OWNER: Quality Assurance
REVIEW FREQUENCY: Annual

1. PURPOSE

This procedure defines how internal quality audits are planned, performed, documented, reported, followed up, and closed.

The objective is to verify whether processes comply with approved procedures, internal controls, quality requirements, and defined acceptance criteria.

2. SCOPE

This procedure applies to:

- Quality Management System processes
- QA and QC activities
- Document control
- Records management
- Approval controls
- CAPA processes
- NCR management
- Inspection and verification activities
- Operational procedures
- Cross-department quality controls

3. AUDIT PRINCIPLES

Internal audits must be:

- Evidence-based
- Objective
- Documented
- Independent where practical
- Risk-focused
- Traceable to defined requirements

Audit conclusions must not be based only on assumptions or verbal statements.

4. AUDIT PLANNING

The audit plan should define:

- Audit scope
- Audit objective
- Process or department
- Audit criteria
- Auditor
- Scheduled date
- Required records
- Previous findings to review

Higher-risk areas may be audited more frequently.

5. AUDIT CRITERIA

Audit criteria may include:

- Approved SOPs
- Work instructions
- Quality policies
- Controlled forms
- Approval matrices
- Contractual requirements
- Internal quality controls
- Previous CAPA commitments
- Defined acceptance criteria

Every audit finding must be linked to an identifiable requirement.

6. AUDIT EVIDENCE

Acceptable evidence may include:

- Approved documents
- System records
- Completed forms
- Logs
- Inspection records
- Approval history
- Training records
- Audit trails
- Process observations
- Interviews supported by objective evidence

Verbal statements alone should not be treated as sufficient evidence when documentary evidence is required.

7. AUDIT FINDING CATEGORIES

Audit findings may be classified as:

A. COMPLIANT

Use when:

- Requirement is fully met.
- Required evidence is available.
- Defined controls are operating as intended.

B. OBSERVATION

Use when:

- No confirmed mandatory requirement has been violated.
- An improvement opportunity exists.
- A potential weakness may create future risk.

C. MINOR NON-CONFORMANCE

Use when:

- An isolated requirement failure exists.
- Scope is limited.
- No significant customer, regulatory, contractual, safety, or systemic risk is identified.

D. MAJOR NON-CONFORMANCE

Use when:

- Significant control failure exists.
- Mandatory approval or critical requirement was bypassed.
- Evidence of compliance is unavailable.
- Failure is repeated or systemic.
- Multiple records or processes are affected.
- Significant customer, regulatory, contractual, or safety risk exists.

Final NCR classification must also follow the approved Non-Conformance Procedure and NCR Severity Classification Matrix.

8. AUDIT SAMPLING

Auditors may use representative samples when full-population review is not practical.

Sample selection should consider:

- Process risk
- Record volume
- Previous findings
- Recent changes
- Known problem areas
- Customer or compliance impact

A clean sample does not automatically prove that the entire process is compliant.

9. PREVIOUS FINDINGS

Auditors should review previous findings when applicable.

The review should determine whether:

- Corrective action was completed.
- CAPA was implemented.
- The issue recurred.
- Effectiveness was verified.
- Similar failures exist elsewhere.

Recurrence of a previously closed issue may justify higher severity or CAPA escalation.

10. AUDIT REPORT

An internal audit report should include:

- Audit reference
- Audit date
- Scope
- Auditor
- Department or process
- Requirements reviewed
- Evidence examined
- Findings
- Classification
- Recommended actions
- Responsible owner
- Due dates
- Escalation requirements

11. FINDING EVIDENCE REQUIREMENT

Every NCR identified during an audit should contain sufficient objective evidence to explain:

- What requirement was not met
- What evidence was reviewed
- What deviation was identified
- What records or processes were affected

The finding description should be factual and reproducible.

12. CORRECTIVE ACTION

Process owners must address confirmed audit NCRs.

Depending on severity, action may include:

- Immediate correction
- Containment
- Root cause analysis
- Procedure revision
- Retraining
- Control improvement
- CAPA initiation
- System change

13. ESCALATION

Escalation to QA Management is required when:

- Major NCR is identified
- Critical evidence is missing
- Same finding has recurred
- Customer impact exists
- Regulatory or contractual exposure exists
- Management control failure is identified
- Multiple departments are affected
- Corrective actions remain overdue
- Previous CAPA was ineffective

14. FOLLOW-UP AUDIT

Follow-up review may be required when:

- Major NCR was raised
- CAPA was initiated
- Finding was repeated
- Corrective action effectiveness must be verified
- Management requests additional assurance

15. AUDIT CLOSURE

An audit may be considered closed when:

- All mandatory findings are assigned.
- Required actions are completed or formally accepted.
- Supporting evidence is available.
- Major NCR effectiveness is verified where required.
- Outstanding risks are escalated appropriately.

16. AUDIT RECORD RETENTION

Audit records may include:

- Audit plan
- Checklist
- Evidence references
- Finding records
- Audit report
- Corrective action evidence
- Follow-up verification
- Closure approval

17. RAG COPILOT DECISION RULE

For audit-related questions, the QA/QC RAG Copilot must:

- Identify the applicable audit requirement.
- Use objective evidence from the approved knowledge base.
- Distinguish compliant conditions from observations and NCRs.
- Refer confirmed NCR classification to the Non-Conformance Procedure.
- Recommend follow-up or escalation when supported by evidence.
- Identify the source document used.
- Avoid inventing audit evidence, previous findings, or approvals.

If evidence is insufficient, respond:

MANUAL QA REVIEW REQUIRED
